# Your work account on your own phone

You can add your work account to a personal phone to get work mail, calendar, and contacts. It is
optional. Here is what happens and what the company can and cannot see.

## Adding the account

**iPhone or iPad.** Settings, Mail (or Accounts), Add Account, Google, and sign in with your work
address. You will be asked to install the Google Device Policy app and to approve a management
profile for the work account; accept both. An administrator approves the device the first time,
usually within a day, and mail starts flowing once approved.

**Android.** Settings, Accounts, Add account, Google, and sign in with your work address. Your
phone creates a separate work profile: a second set of apps with a briefcase badge, where work
mail and files live apart from your personal ones. An administrator approves the device the first
time.

A screen lock is required on the phone; the setup asks for one if you have none.

## What the company can see and do

- It can see the device model, its operating system version, and whether it has a screen lock.
- It can remove the work account and the work data from the phone, for example when you leave or
  if the phone is lost. On Android that is the work profile; on an iPhone it is the work account
  and its mail, calendar, and contacts.
- It cannot see your personal apps, photos, messages, browsing, or location, and it cannot wipe
  the whole phone.

## Removing the account yourself

iPhone: Settings, General, VPN & Device Management, remove the work profile, then remove the
account under Mail. Android: Settings, Accounts, remove the work profile. Your personal data is
untouched either way. Tell IT if you remove it so the device is cleared from the inventory.

## Not a phone the company issued

A company-owned iPad is managed differently (it is enrolled as a company device and can be locked
or erased remotely); you will know, because IT handed it to you. This article is about your own
phone.
